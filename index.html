    1 · Side walk

    Gait speed · step length · step height

    Not recorded
    Do several passes in one recording. A pass is one walk all the way across the camera view. Each pass adds 4–6 measured steps, so more passes give more reliable averages and balanced left/right values.
    1. Start recording, then walk across the view (pass 1).
    2. Keep walking about 2 m past the edge of the view, turn around out of view, and walk back across (pass 2).
    3. Turn again out of view and walk across once more (pass 3). Stop recording.
    Minimum 1 pass · recommended 3–4 passes (about 30–40 seconds) · up to 6 is fine.
    How to set up
    1. Turn the phone sideways (landscape). Fix it at about hip height, level, 3–4 m from the walking line and pointing straight at it.
    2. Clear a straight walkway of about 8 m. The person starts and stops about 2 m outside the camera view so they are walking at a steady pace while in view.
    3. Walk at a comfortable, everyday speed. Turns should happen outside the camera view.
    4. Only one person in view. Good lighting. Fitted clothing helps (loose trousers or skirts hide the legs).

    2 · Walk and turn

    Base width · steps to turn · turn time

    Not recorded
    Optional: repeat for better accuracy. Every 180° turn in the recording is measured and averaged, and base width uses all the straight walking.
    1. Start recording. Walk from the start line to the far marker and turn around (turn 1).
    2. Walk back to the start line (about 2 m from the phone) and turn around again (turn 2).
    3. Walk to the far marker, turn (turn 3) and walk back. Stop recording.
    Minimum 1 turn · recommended 2–3 turns. Keep the whole body in view, including at the start line.
    How to set up
    1. Hold the phone upright (portrait). Fix it at about hip height at one end of the walkway, pointing straight along it.
    2. Put a marker on the floor about 6 m from the phone, and a start line about 2 m from the phone. The person starts at the start line, facing away.
    3. Walk to the marker at a comfortable speed, turn around (180°), and walk back toward the phone. Stop at the start line (or turn again to repeat).
    4. Walk straight toward the middle of the camera view. Keep the whole body, including the feet, in view the entire time.

    About GaitCam

    GaitCam measures gait speed, step length, step height, base width and steps to turn from a phone video, and compares each value with typical ranges for healthy adults.

    • Private by design: video is analysed on the phone with MediaPipe Pose and is never uploaded. Saved results stay in this browser only.
    • One phone: a side-walk test (speed, step length, step height) and a walk-and-turn test (base width, steps to turn).
    • Not a diagnosis: GaitCam is a research and clinical support tool. It is not a medical device and has not yet been validated against an instrumented walkway or motion capture. Values outside a typical range are a prompt to look closer.

    Source code: github.com/ts-iowa/gaitcam ·

    How the reference ranges are used

    ParameterTypical range in GaitCamRefs
    Gait speedMean for age decade and sex, −0.2 to +0.25 m/s. Notes below 1.0 m/s (higher risk of falls and adverse outcomes) and below 0.8 m/s (limited community walking).1–5
    Step length≈ 0.37–0.47 × height under age 60; 0.33–0.45 at 60–79; 0.28–0.42 at 80+ (approximate).6
    Step length symmetryLonger ÷ shorter side ≤ 1.10.11
    Step heightPeak heel lift in swing ≈ 0.10–0.18 × height (approximate; limited normative data for video).10
    Base width5–14 cm (community-dwelling adults ≈ 10 ± 4 cm).7
    Steps to turn 180°≤ 4 steps; more steps is a common trigger for balance and fall-risk assessment.8, 9
    Cadence≈ 100–125 steps/min at comfortable speed.12

    A change in gait speed of about 0.05 m/s is considered small but meaningful, and about 0.1 m/s substantial (ref 5), which is useful when comparing repeat assessments.
